The problem

Every tool organises your mail. None of them think about it.

Filters, labels, snoozes, priority inboxes, AI that writes replies faster — the entire category is built around moving messages out of sight more efficiently. It works, in the narrow sense. The inbox gets emptier. But nothing in that stack has ever asked the question you actually carry around all day: what does all of this mean, and what does it mean for this week?

So the real work stays with you. You are the one holding the fact that you promised someone a document on Monday, that a negotiation has gone quiet for nine days in a way that should worry you, that the thing due Friday needs four uninterrupted hours you have not scheduled and will not get if the week continues as it is. That knowledge lives in your head, degrades under load, and surfaces at 2am rather than at the moment it would have been useful.

Sovereign is built for that layer instead. It reads the same inbox and the same calendar as everything else, and then does the part nobody has automated: it works out what is actually happening across your commitments, your relationships and your time — and tells you, once, each morning, in plain language.

Privacy

Built so we couldn't read your data even if we wanted to

Not a policy promise layered over a cloud. A property of where the data sits.

Everything lives on your machine

Your mail, your calendar, your commitments, your scores — all of it is stored locally on your own computer. There is no copy of your inbox on our servers, because there are no servers holding user content at all.

Your credentials never leave the device

The keys to your mailbox are held in your operating system's own secure credential store and are never transmitted to us, never written into a file you could accidentally share, and never visible to anyone but you.

It cannot destroy anything

Sovereign reads your mail, and — only when you tell it to — archives a thread or sends a reply you have approved. It has no ability to delete a message or empty a mailbox, and it never asks for the permission that would let it.

Nothing reports back

There is no usage analytics, no behavioural telemetry, no quiet stream of what you opened and when. Nobody — not us, not an employer, not an administrator — gets a dashboard of how you spend your day.

No advertising. Ever.

Your data will not be sold, brokered, or used to train anyone's models, and there will never be an advertising surface in this product. You pay for the software, so the software has no reason to want anything else from you.

Delete the app and it is all gone

Erasure is a single gesture on your own computer, not a support ticket and a 30-day wait. When the app is gone, the data is gone, with nothing left behind on our side to request, verify, or take on trust.

The one thing that does leave — stated plainly

Sovereign uses AI for a handful of things: the morning brief, situation synthesis, drafting. When you ask for one of those, the request it sends contains text drawn from your mail, and that request travels off your machine to reach an AI model. We route it through a privacy relay that strips identifying details on the way, and it is never stored — but we are not going to pretend it never happens.

So the honest version is this: local-first does not mean nothing ever leaves. It means your data is never stored anywhere but your own machine, nothing leaves without you asking for it, and most of what Sovereign does — the relationship signals, the ranking, the silence detection, the scoring — is worked out on your device and never leaves at all.

間
The Ma principle

The space between is the architecture, not the leftovers

Ma is the Japanese idea of intentional negative space — the charged interval that gives the surrounding form its shape. Sovereign treats your unscheduled time exactly that way: not as a gap waiting to be filled, but as the thing that makes the rest of the work possible.

In practice this means the system defends time you would give away. A deadline that lands on a Saturday does not get scheduled onto your weekend; it gets routed back into the week, with quick work pulled to Friday and demanding work placed where you will still have the capacity for it. Rest is not a setting you enable — it is a boundary the system holds even when you would not.

And because it is structural rather than cosmetic, it is measured. Sovereign tracks whether your protected time actually stayed protected, and only credits you when there was real pressure to protect it against. An empty week does not score as a well-defended one.

What you deliberately leave out — of the weekend, of the cloud — is the architecture, not the absence of it.
